# Importance assessment

Selected category: **Other**. The scope is a controlled physical comparison for material handling; no broad occupational-coverage claim is made.

The real-world connection is robot-run physical comparison for material handling. ASTM G219's public significance summary identifies inclined-plane testing as relevant to sliding components and chute design. G219 measures static breakaway friction. This task uses a fixed 30-degree ramp and relative runout stopping distances, so the submitted procedure is different and makes no standards-compliance claim. [Public scope and significance](https://store.astm.org/g0219-23.html)

Both specimens have a common ramp geometry and release gate. The robot must physically arrange them, initiate the comparison without hand assistance during measurement, observe stopping outcomes, and place the selected specimen. The demonstrated expert reads simulator poses and measured outcomes. That privileged feedback is disclosed. It never reads the friction assignments. Contact pushing recovers final placement.

The narrow scope is relative sliding behaviour for the specified synthetic material pairs and layout. It does not certify slip resistance, quantify a real material coefficient, establish a calibrated tribometer, or demonstrate transfer to arbitrary specimens. Fixed positions and assignments also leave generalisation untested.

Other is an explicit guide category for additional domains informative about real-world impact. It still requires distinctiveness and substantive relevance. This case is a physical experimental-reasoning test with a material-handling motivation; it is not evidence of large-scale job impact. [Guide](https://tasks.robocurve.org/#rubric)

Residual review risk: organisers may judge this experiment too elementary or too narrow even under Other. The cited evidence establishes a relevant application family, not guaranteed importance-bar acceptance. Changing category does not cure an inadequate task.
